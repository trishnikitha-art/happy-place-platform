## Materials Terminology

### Wood Species (Pacific Northwest Native)

**Western Red Cedar**
- Scientific name: Thuja plicata
- Characteristics: Naturally rot-resistant, lightweight, straight grain
- Uses: Decks, fencing, siding, outdoor furniture
- Lifespan: 20-30 years (untreated), 40+ years (maintained)
- Cost: Premium ($8-12/linear ft for 2x6)
- Maintenance: Annual cleaning, restaining every 3-5 years

**Douglas Fir**
- Scientific name: Pseudotsuga menziesii
- Characteristics: Strong, stiff, moderate rot resistance
- Uses: Framing, structural beams, flooring
- Lifespan: 50+ years (protected), 15-20 years (exposed)
- Cost: Mid-range ($4-6/linear ft for 2x6)
- Maintenance: Minimal if protected

**Western Hemlock**
- Scientific name: Tsuga heterophylla
- Characteristics: Fair strength, poor rot resistance
- Uses: Interior framing, plywood, pallets
- Lifespan: 20+ years (protected)
- Cost: Budget ($3-4/linear ft for 2x6)
- Maintenance: Must be protected from moisture

**Redwood**
- Scientific name: Sequoia sempervirens
- Characteristics: Excellent rot resistance, beautiful grain
- Uses: Decks, siding, outdoor furniture
- Lifespan: 25-30 years (untreated), 50+ years (maintained)
- Cost: Premium ($10-15/linear ft for 2x6)
- Maintenance: Annual cleaning, restaining every 2-3 years

**Pressure-Treated Pine**
- Chemical treatment: ACQ (alkaline copper quaternary)
- Characteristics: Rot-resistant, green tint
- Uses: Ground contact, posts, structural framing
- Lifespan: 20-40 years (depending on treatment level)
- Cost: Budget ($3-5/linear ft for 2x6)
- Maintenance: None required, but can be stained

### Composite Materials

**Trex/Composite Decking**
- Composition: Recycled plastic + wood fibers
- Characteristics: Rot-resistant, low maintenance
- Lifespan: 25-30 years
- Cost: Premium ($12-18/linear ft)
- Maintenance: Annual cleaning, no staining

**PVC Decking**
- Composition: 100% PVC plastic
- Characteristics: Waterproof, mold-resistant
- Lifespan: 30+ years
- Cost: Premium ($15-25/linear ft)
- Maintenance: Annual cleaning

## Trade Vocabulary

### Joinery Terms

**Mortise and Tenon**
- Definition: Rectangular hole (mortise) + protruding end (tenon)
- Usage: Furniture, timber framing
- Strength: Excellent
- Complexity: High

**Dovetail Joint**
- Definition: Interlocking trapezoidal pins and tails
- Usage: Drawers, boxes, fine furniture
- Strength: Excellent (mechanical lock)
- Complexity: High

**Rabbet Joint**
- Definition: L-shaped cut along edge
- Usage: Cabinet backs, door frames
- Strength: Good
- Complexity: Low

**Butt Joint**
- Definition: Two pieces joined end-to-end
- Usage: Basic framing, simple projects
- Strength: Weak (requires reinforcement)
- Complexity: Low

**Pocket Hole Joint**
- Definition: Angled hole + screw
- Usage: Face frames, cabinet assembly
- Strength: Good
- Complexity: Low

**Miter Joint**
- Definition: Two pieces cut at 45° angles
- Usage: Picture frames, trim, molding
- Strength: Weak (requires reinforcement)
- Complexity: Medium

### Fasteners

**Galvanized Nails**
- Coating: Zinc coating
- Usage: Exterior applications, roofing
- Lifespan: 10-15 years (coating wears off)

**Stainless Steel Screws**
- Grade: 305 (outdoor), 316 (marine)
- Usage: Exterior, coastal areas
- Lifespan: 50+ years

**Hex Bolts**
- Usage: Structural connections, timber framing
- Strength: Very high

**Carriage Bolts**
- Usage: Wood-to-wood connections
- Strength: High

**Lag Screws**
- Usage: Heavy-duty wood connections
- Strength: Very high

### Finishing Terms

**Stain**
- Purpose: Color + protection
- Types: Transparent, semi-transparent, solid
- Reapplication: Every 2-5 years

**Sealant**
- Purpose: Waterproofing protection
- Types: Water-based, oil-based
- Reapplication: Every 1-3 years

**Varnish**
- Purpose: Clear protective coating
- Types: Spar (outdoor), interior
- Reapplication: Every 3-5 years

**Paint**
- Purpose: Color + protection
- Types: Latex (water-based), oil-based
- Reapplication: Every 5-10 years

## Common Homeowner Mistakes

### Deck Mistakes

**1. Inadequate Footings**
- Mistake: Using concrete blocks instead of poured footings
- Consequence: Deck settles, becomes unstable
- Correct: 12" diameter footings below frost line (18" in Oregon)

**2. Poor Flashing**
- Mistake: No flashing at ledger board
- Consequence: Water damage to house structure
- Correct: Metal flashing behind ledger board

**3. Wrong Fasteners**
- Mistake: Using interior nails for exterior
- Consequence: Rust, structural failure
- Correct: Galvanized or stainless steel fasteners

**4. Inadequate Spacing**
- Mistake: Joists too far apart
- Consequence: Sagging, bouncy deck
- Correct: 16" on center for 2x6, 12" for 2x8

**5. No Ventilation**
- Mistake: Sealing deck completely
- Consequence: Trapped moisture, rot
- Correct: Gap between deck boards (1/8")

### Fence Mistakes

**1. Posts Not Set Deep Enough**
- Mistake: Shallow post holes
- Consequence: Fence leans, falls over
- Correct: 24-36" deep, below frost line

**2. Wrong Post Spacing**
- Mistake: Posts too far apart
- Consequence: Sagging panels
- Correct: 6-8' on center

**3. No Gravel Drainage**
- Mistake: Posts set directly in soil
- Consequence: Rot at base
- Correct: 6" gravel at bottom of hole

**4. Poor Gate Hardware**
- Mistake: Lightweight hinges
- Consequence: Gate sags, doesn't close
- Correct: Heavy-duty gate hardware

### Pergola Mistakes

**1. Insufficient Bracing**
- Mistake: No diagonal bracing
- Consequence: Structure sways, unstable
- Correct: Cross-bracing or knee braces

**2. Wrong Beam Size**
- Mistake: Beams too small for span
- Consequence: Sagging, structural failure
- Correct: Proper engineering for span

**3. Poor Attachment**
- Mistake: Nailing instead of bolting
- Consequence: Structure fails under load
- Correct: Lag bolts or through-bolts

## Common Contractor Shortcuts

### Material Substitutions

**1. Using Lower-Grade Lumber**
- Shortcut: Using #2 grade instead of #1
- Issue: More knots, warping, structural weakness
- Correct: Use specified grade

**2. Thinner Decking**
- Shortcut: 5/4" instead of 2" decking
- Issue: More flexing, shorter lifespan
- Correct: Use specified thickness

**3. Fewer Joists**
- Shortcut: 24" spacing instead of 16"
- Issue: Bouncy deck, sagging
- Correct: Follow spacing requirements

### Installation Shortcuts

**1. Skipping Flashing**
- Shortcut: No ledger board flashing
- Issue: Water damage to house
- Correct: Always install flashing

**2. Insufficient Fasteners**
- Shortcut: Fewer nails/screws than specified
- Issue: Structural failure
- Correct: Follow fastener schedule

**3. Poor Fastener Type**
- Shortcut: Using cheaper fasteners
- Issue: Rust, failure
- Correct: Use specified fasteners

### Finish Shortcuts

**1. Skipping Primer**
- Shortcut: No primer before paint
- Issue: Poor adhesion, peeling
- Correct: Always use primer

**2. Single Coat**
- Shortcut: One coat instead of two
- Issue: Poor coverage, shorter lifespan
- Correct: Apply specified number of coats

**3. Wrong Product**
- Shortcut: Interior paint for exterior
- Issue: Premature failure
- Correct: Use exterior-rated products

## Maintenance Terminology

### Annual Maintenance

**Deck**
- Sweep and clean debris
- Inspect for loose boards, fasteners
- Check for rot, mold
- Restain if needed (every 2-3 years)

**Fence**
- Inspect for loose posts, panels
- Check for rot at base
- Clean mold/mildew
- Restain if needed (every 3-5 years)

**Pergola**
- Inspect for loose connections
- Check for rot, insect damage
- Clean debris
- Restain if needed (every 3-5 years)

### Seasonal Maintenance

**Spring**
- Clean winter debris
- Inspect for winter damage
- Check for mold/mildew
- Repair any damage

**Fall**
- Clear leaves, debris
- Inspect for summer damage
- Prepare for winter
- Apply protective coating if needed

**Winter**
- Remove snow load
- Inspect for ice damage
- Check for water infiltration
- Plan spring repairs

## Structural Terms

### Load-Bearing

**Dead Load**
- Definition: Weight of structure itself
- Example: Deck weight, furniture weight
- Typical: 10-15 psf (pounds per square foot)

**Live Load**
- Definition: Weight of people, movable objects
- Example: People on deck, snow load
- Typical: 40-50 psf for residential decks

**Total Load**
- Definition: Dead load + live load
- Typical: 50-65 psf for residential decks

### Span Ratings

**2x6 Joists**
- 16" OC: 9' span
- 24" OC: 7' span

**2x8 Joists**
- 16" OC: 12' span
- 24" OC: 10' span

**2x10 Joists**
- 16" OC: 15' span
- 24" OC: 13' span

## Measurement Terminology

**Board Foot**
- Definition: 144 cubic inches (1" x 12" x 12")
- Usage: Lumber pricing

**Linear Foot**
- Definition: 12 inches of length
- Usage: Trim, molding pricing

**Square Foot**
- Definition: 12" x 12" area
- Usage: Decking, flooring pricing

**On Center (OC)**
- Definition: Distance from center of one member to center of next
- Usage: Joist spacing, stud spacing

## Oregon-Specific Considerations

### Building Codes

**Oregon Residential Specialty Code (ORSC)**
- Deck railing height: 36" minimum
- Baluster spacing: 4" maximum
- Stair riser height: 7.75" maximum
- Stair tread depth: 10" minimum

### Permits

**When Permits Required**
- Decks over 30" above grade
- Fences over 6' tall
- Pergolas attached to house
- Any structural modifications

**Permit Process**
- Submit plans to local jurisdiction
- Plan review (1-2 weeks)
- Inspection during construction
- Final inspection required

## Quality Indicators

### Good Craftsmanship Signs

**Joints**
- Tight, no gaps
- Properly aligned
- Reinforced where needed

**Fasteners**
- Correct type for application
- Properly spaced
- Countersunk or flush
- No rust

**Finish**
- Even coverage
- No drips, runs
- Proper preparation
- Compatible products

**Structure**
- Level, plumb, square
- Proper support
- No sagging
- Adequate bracing

**Joints**
- Gaps, misalignment
- No reinforcement
- Poor fit

**Fasteners**
- Wrong type
- Too few
- Protruding
- Rusting

**Finish**
- Uneven coverage
- Drips, runs
- Poor preparation
- Wrong products

**Structure**
- Not level/plumb
- Inadequate support
- Sagging
- No bracing

## Educational Opportunities

### What Homeowners Should Know

**Wood Movement**
- Wood expands and contracts with humidity
- Allow for movement in construction
- Acclimate wood before installation

**Moisture Content**
- Ideal: 12-15% for interior use
- Test with moisture meter
- Store properly before use

**Rot Prevention**
- Keep wood dry
- Provide ventilation
- Use rot-resistant species
- Apply protective finishes

**Fastener Selection**
- Use correct type for application
- Consider environment (coastal vs inland)
- Use corrosion-resistant fasteners outdoors

### What Contractors Should Explain

**Material Choices**
- Why specific materials chosen
- Pros and cons of alternatives
- Maintenance requirements
- Lifespan expectations

**Construction Methods**
- Why specific techniques used
- Code requirements
- Best practices
- Industry standards

**Maintenance Requirements**
- What maintenance is needed
- How often
- How to perform
- Cost implications
